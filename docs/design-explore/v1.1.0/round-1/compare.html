Splity Tab v1.1.0, design round 1

Five directions for the key screen, each drawn by its own designer from the same brief. Every frame below is the real prototype, so you can switch Even and By item, open the spare sen choice, press New bill and Copy summary, and use Prototype states inside it for the other states. The desktop view is further down. Every direction shows the brief's exact figures, checked by its own script.

My pick, D Share Orbit

The key screen's job is shares everyone trusts and that visibly add back up to the total. D draws exactly that, a ring around the total where each person owns a segment as long as their share, so "it adds up" is a shape and not only a sum. It also shows this version's new feature, the spare sen, with a mark in the ring and "+1 sen" beside the share that took it. On a phone the answer comes first, and on desktop the ring with name callouts is the most memorable screen of the five. Dark charcoal, one coral and a muted colour per person keep the Discord feel.

Runner-up A, Night Console, the most literally Discord, with the shares arriving as a bot message with @mentions. Pick it if you want both apps to look like one family, since my Streaks pick is A too. Its cost is that the shares sit one scroll below the inputs on a phone.

Decisions for you

1. Which direction. Pick one, ask for a mix (it becomes one new direction), or reject them all (round 2 brings new bets, not polished copies).

2. Only if you pick D. Its "+1 sen" label beside a share is not in the brief. A and C suggest the same thing in their notes. I recommend keeping it, because it makes your spare sen choice visible without comparing numbers.

Direction A, runner-up

Night Console

A chat app's dark console. People read like a member list, items are tapped like reactions, and the shares arrive as a message from the Splity Tab bot.

Bets on
Anyone who uses Discord or Telegram reads it at once. The bot message makes the result an answer, and its proof row shows the working. Tapping who had an item feels like adding a reaction.
Costs
It reads as "Discord-like" before it reads as its own brand. On a phone the shares are one scroll below the inputs.

Prototype only. "At random" uses a fixed seed, so the same draw comes back on reload. The build uses a real random pick.

Direction B

Glass Receipt

The receipt becomes one floating holographic card, the only glass on the page, with the total softly lit.

Bets on
The answer comes first, and the lit total is what a visitor remembers. One material and one light carry all the spice, the rest is plain navy. Each share reads like a receipt line, which fits 360 px at any amount.
Costs
On a phone the inputs sit under the card. Glass and glow are easy to overdo, so they are rationed to three places.

Prototype only. The Prototype states tab covers a name field on a phone, and "At random" uses a fixed seed.

Direction C

Daylight Instrument

Futuristic in daylight. The bill reads like a precision instrument, with the total in oversized thin numerals.

Bets on
The numerals are the one bold thing. A share scale under the readout is the logo's cut bar drawn to the bill's real proportions. The whole answer fits the first phone screen.
Costs
A light page is the furthest from "like discord". The thin figures need a system font with a light weight, and look heavier without one.

Prototype only. "At random" uses a fixed seed and steers its first draw away from the top of the list, so it is not a true random pick. The build uses a real one.

Direction D, my pick

Share Orbit

The split is a shape. A ring of segments around the total, one per person, each as long as that person's share.

Bets on
"It adds up" becomes something you see. Coral marks the spare sen in the ring and beside the share. A muted colour per person ties the ring, the rows and the callouts together. The arcs match the shares exactly.
Costs
On a phone the ring comes first, so you scroll to type. Twelve people make thin segments, which carry initials to stay readable.

Not in the brief. The "+1 sen" label beside a share. Decision 2 above.

Direction E

Command Line

A keyboard tool for splitting a bill. Mono columns line up like terminal output, and the items list is one command palette.

Bets on
The only light direction with a warm accent, and the only one where the input is the memorable object. Every action shows its key, and every action still works by tap.
Costs
Calmer than "like discord". Key caps are detail a phone user never presses. On a phone the shares sit below the inputs.

Not in the brief. The Items and Charges columns drop "RM" to fit 360 px, while the brief writes money as "RM 38.87". The people picker also shows each person's share.
