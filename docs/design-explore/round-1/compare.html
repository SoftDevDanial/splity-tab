Split, design round 1

Three directions for the key screen, drawn independently from the same brief. Each frame is the phone view at 390 x 844 and scrolls. Open one full size to see the desktop layout.

Recommendation

C, Working shown. The screen has one job, which is to make the shares believable without anyone doing maths. C is the only direction where the proof is on the screen: each charge shows its base (6% of RM 65.34, so SST on subtotal plus service is visible), and the shares table adds across (Items + Charges = Share) and down to a check row that lands on the receipt total. A is the most charming but its monospace receipt makes a long page longer and its proof is two matching frames, not a sum. B is friendly and its stacked bar is a nice touch, but colour per person stops scaling past six people and the bar shows proportions, not cents. One change before locking C: the amounts printed inside the item toggles (Aisyah RM 9.00) are not in the brainstorm, so they come out and go to the suggestions note.

Direction A

Receipt tape

The receipt you already hold, made to add up.

Bets on
Matching the paper in their hand. Total and the Adds up to line share one frame and one column, so the match is visible.
Costs
Monospace density makes the page about 1680 px long on a phone. One-letter stamps clash when two names share an initial.

Direction B

Colour per person

Who had what, visible at a glance.

Bets on
One colour per person across toggles, a stacked bar of the total, and coloured share rows.
Costs
Six colours repeat for people 7 to 12. Teal and blue look alike to colour-blind users, so names carry the meaning anyway. The bar shows proportions, not cents.

Direction C · recommended

Working shown

A calculator that shows its working.

Bets on
Trust from visible arithmetic. Plus and equals signs in a fixed column, each charge shows its base, the shares table sums across and down to the total.
Costs
Leans toward accounting software. The shares table is tight at 360 px. Charge rates are edited inside the summary, which mixes inputs into the output.
